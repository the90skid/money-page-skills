# Build Your Money Page — ChatGPT Prompt

Copy everything below the line into ChatGPT (with web browsing on). At the end, ChatGPT hands you a complete HTML page. If ChatGPT is connected to your Shopify store, it can create the page for you as a hidden draft; otherwise paste it in yourself: Online Store → Pages → Add page → click the `</>` (HTML) view → paste → Save. Keep the page hidden until the checklist passes.

---

You are building one money page for me, an ecommerce founder, end to end: research → wireframe → copy → final HTML → ship checklist → first test. A money page is a standalone page between a paid ad and the product page, with no navigation and one job: take a cold visitor from curious to convinced, then send them forward. It is not a homepage and not a product page.

RULES, NON-NEGOTIABLE: Every claim on the page must trace to your research or my answers. Never invent reviews, review counts, numbers, studies, experts, stories or press. If proof for a section is missing, mark it [MISSING: …] and ask me for it, or cut the section. If the reference page leans on proof I don't have, do not imitate the claim; rebuild that section around the proof I do have.

STEP 1 — Ask me for: (1) my product page URL and store URL, (2) the ad (copy or idea) that will point at this page, (3) the format (article / listicle / quiz / offer page) and angle (persona / mechanism / switch / trend / desire / enemy / story / authority / occasion / comparison) I'm building, and the specific customer it's for, (4) my reference page: a link and a full-page screenshot of a money page I want to model (for example from the Funnel Vault), (5) where the button should go: my product page, or a cart link (mystore.com/cart/VARIANT_ID:1) that drops the product straight into checkout. Then browse my product page, homepage and reviews. Extract: what the product is, price, variants, what makes it different, all visible proof, current offers, and the exact words customers use. Summarize; ask me to correct.

STEP 2 — The wireframe. From my reference page, extract its skeleton, never its words or design. Preserve the section order, visual rhythm, proof placement, offer placement and CTA structure. For every section show: its purpose, the content it needs, the proof it requires, the recommended visual, and the CTA placement. Adapt it to my product, customer and angle. Test for the finished page: the original brand would recognize their structure, but not one sentence of the copy. If I have no reference, use the standard skeleton for my format:
- Article: headline that promises information → story that builds a problem the reader didn't know they had → why it happens → product revealed as the answer → proof → offer.
- Listicle: 6-12 numbered points in the buyer's worry order, most important problem near the top, each = claim + why + proof → offer. Six strong reasons beat twelve padded ones.
- Quiz: do not hand-build quiz logic. Recommend a Shopify quiz app and write for me: the questions, answer options, proof screens between questions (ask → prove → ask → prove), and the results-page copy. Then skip to the checklist.
- Offer page: the offer as the headline → what you get, itemized with real value → the honest reason the deal exists → proof → claim it. No fake timers.
Shape it with my angle: Persona (name the reader, their worries in order, reviews only from people like them) · Mechanism (old way vs new way, one nameable difference) · Switch · Trend · Desire · Enemy (true points only) · Story (real person only) · Authority (real, named expert only) · Occasion (a real date) · Comparison (provable facts only). One page, one angle.
Do not write copy yet. Show me the wireframe and wait for my OK.

STEP 3 — The argument, then the copy. First state the buying argument in 6 lines: the problem this customer already recognizes, why their current solution falls short, why my product fits better, the distinct reasons to believe it with the evidence for each, the objections to answer, and the offer it leads to. Then write every section of the wireframe: headline, body copy, proof, recommended visual, CTA. Copy rules: the page's first words keep my ad's exact promise. Plain language, short sentences, easy to scan on mobile. Specific beats impressive ("4.8 stars from 3,214 reviews," not "loved by thousands"). Use my customers' own review words. Announcement bar at the top with the offer if one exists. Repeat the CTA after the problem, after the proof, and at the end; de-risk at the buy moment (guarantee, cancel anytime). No fake urgency. No navigation, no menu, no footer links, no "you might also like." Ask for my edits. Do not produce HTML until I approve the copy. After I approve, offer ONE alternative first screen (headline + hero + first proof) on a different angle, as my first A/B test.

STEP 4 — Brand and images. Ask if I have a brand file or design system (colors, fonts, button style, voice). If not, look at my homepage and note primary color, accent color, heading and body fonts, button shape, and voice. Images, in this order: (1) reuse image URLs from my own product page and store; (2) ask me once for images (ad creatives, UGC, press screenshots); (3) for any section still missing an image, insert a clearly marked placeholder with what it must show, a ready-to-paste AI image prompt, and the size. Never use the reference brand's images. AI images only for scenery, never for evidence (no fake product photos, customers, or before/afters).

STEP 5 — Produce the final page as one clean, self-contained HTML block (inline CSS, mobile first: short paragraphs, images legible at 375px wide, buttons full-width on mobile). Every CTA links to the destination I gave you, never the homepage. No header, menu or footer inside the page. Tell me: a normal Shopify page shows my store's menu and footer around this HTML, so for a true one-exit page I should ask my theme developer or Claude to add a menu-free page template on a duplicate theme, or host the page as a standalone page.

STEP 6 — Walk me through the ship checklist: (1) open the page on my phone: does the first screen keep the ad's exact promise? (2) every claim is true and I can back it up; (3) no image placeholders remain and no image belongs to another brand; (4) one clear CTA, repeated, no menu, no dead links; (5) the click after the CTA keeps the same promise (product page or checkout, never the homepage); (6) my pixel/analytics is on the page; (7) I click through and pretend to buy, and the offer works end to end.

STEP 7 — My first test. Tell me to duplicate my best ad and change only the destination: one copy to this page, one to my current product page (or use Meta's A/B test to split evenly). Run at least 7 days, judge on cost per purchase, don't kill either side before it has spent 2-3× my target cost per purchase, only call a winner on a clear gap (roughly 20% or more) that still holds a few days later, compare purchase counts, not just percentages, and log the result in a four-line entry (Date, Hypothesis, What was done, Outcome), win or lose.

Start with Step 1 now.
