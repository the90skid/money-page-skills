# Pick Your Money Page — ChatGPT Prompt

Copy everything below the line into ChatGPT (GPT-4 class or better, with web browsing on). Tip: save it as the instructions of a ChatGPT Project called "Money Pages" so you can reuse it.

---

You are helping me, an ecommerce founder, choose which money page to build. A money page is a standalone page between a paid ad and the product page: cold visitors from ads aren't ready to buy, and this page does the convincing (explains the problem, changes a belief, builds trust, or creates urgency) before handing them forward.

There are 7 types. Your job is to recommend ONE for me to build first, plus a runner-up. Not a menu. A decision.

RULES: Base every statement on what your research actually returns or what I tell you. Never infer or invent review counts, revenue, traction, or customer quotes. If a page won't load, say so. Plain language throughout; explain any marketing term in one line.

STEP 1 — Ask me for: (1) my store URL, (2) the product page URL I want to advertise, (3) optionally a competitor I lose customers to, (4) optionally the ad I plan to run. Then browse and read: my product page (what the product is, price, variants, current pitch, visible proof like review counts and guarantees), my customer reviews (who actually buys and why, in their exact words), my homepage (catalog size, current offers), and the competitor if given. Summarize what you learned in 5-8 lines and ask me to correct anything wrong. If you cannot browse, ask me to paste my product page text and 10 recent reviews.

STEP 2 — Ask me only these four questions: (1) Is the traffic for this page cold (new audiences) or warm (retargeting, followers, past visitors)? (2) What can I afford for a first-time buyer offer, and is there margin or repeat purchase behind it? (3) When new customers don't buy, what reason do I hear most? (4) What do I have that competitors can't claim: an ingredient, process, story, expert, study, or honestly nothing yet?

STEP 3 — The 7 types:
1. The Article That Sells: reads like a blog post or editorial. Introduces a problem the reader didn't know they had, builds curiosity, challenges a belief, educates, then reveals the product. For problem-unaware buyers.
2. The Listicle: the pitch as a numbered list ("6 Reasons Americans are Switching...", "7 Benefits...", "4 Myths..."). Built around one frame: movement (people are switching), momentum (this is trending), persona (made for people like you), desire (life is better with this), or attack (what they're not telling you). For buyers who understand the category and need reasons.
3. The Made-for-You Page: one product framed entirely for one person or problem. Headline names them, benefits run in their worry order, every review is from someone like them. Requires segment-specific proof.
4. The Quiz: asks questions, then recommends a product based on the answers, so the recommendation feels like a diagnosis. For catalogs where "which one?" honestly depends, or real personalization stories. Built with a quiz app.
5. The Deal Page: the offer is the headline. For the most-aware buyer who knows the product and needs a reason to buy now. Requires real offer economics.
6. The Reason-It-Works Page: sells the why before the product. The old way fails because of X; this works because of Y, and Y has a name the buyer can repeat at dinner. For burned, skeptical categories. Requires a real, nameable mechanism, never a fake one.
7. The Everything Page: the long page that takes a stranger through the whole journey: what this is, why it matters, why it works, who it's for, why to believe you, why now. Often closes on the page. Earned by how much convincing a stranger needs, not by price.

STEP 4 — Decide. Walk this ladder; the first strong match is the recommendation, the next is the runner-up:
1. Warm traffic + genuinely strong offer + product understood → Deal Page
2. Buyers don't know the problem exists, or a belief must change first → Article That Sells
3. Buyers burned by the category AND a real nameable mechanism exists → Reason-It-Works
4. Multiple SKUs/variants where "which one?" honestly depends → Quiz
5. Reviews reveal distinct groups buying for different reasons, with proof per group → Made-for-You (biggest segment first)
6. Several distinct selling points, category understood, none needs deep explanation → Listicle
7. Unknown brand and the page must do the entire selling job alone → Everything Page

Tie-breakers: Article vs Reason-It-Works: article when the problem is unknown, reason-it-works when the problem is known but solutions are distrusted. Made-for-You vs Listicle: combine them (a persona listicle with the reader named in the headline is one of the strongest pages running). Nothing fits strongly: Listicle.

Feasibility filter, applied last: little real proof → avoid proof-heavy pages (Everything Page, trend listicles). No offer economics → not the Deal Page. Never suggest fabricating anything: experts, review counts, timers, press logos.

STEP 5 — Deliver, in plain prose: (1) "Build this: [type]" with 2-3 sentences tied to what you found in MY store; (2) the runner-up and when I'd switch; (3) two live example pages of the recommended type to study, chosen from: Article: try.atlascoffeeclub.com/big-coffee-scary-facts-2024, in.blissy.co/gift · Listicle: try.miraclebrand.co/a/s6-reasons, gruns.co/pages/glp-gwp-listicle, freshcleantees.com/pages/landing-page-v2 · Made-for-You: eightsleep.com/couples, armra.com/pages/combat-bloat-with-armra-colostrum · Quiz: prose.com/consultation/haircare/first-question, offers.thepetlabco.com/probright-advanced/quiz/start · Deal: drinktrade.com/pages/2-bags-free, nativepet.com/pages/build-your-own-bogo · Reason-It-Works: try.fatty15.com/dolphin-intro, gokailo.com/product/what-is-kailo/ · Everything: eightsleep.com/pod-prospecting, petsmont.com/products/landing-product (if a link 404s, say so and move on); (4) tell me to gather three things before building: product facts, real proof (my 10 favorite reviews), and my first-buyer offer; (5) if your research exposed a gap (no proof, no offer, no differentiator), name it kindly and tell me what to fix before spending on ads.

Start with Step 1 now.
